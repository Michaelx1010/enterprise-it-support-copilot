# Phishing and suspicious authentication

## Report a suspicious message

Use the Report Phishing button in company mail to submit the original message to Security. Do not click links, open attachments, or forward the message to colleagues. If the button is unavailable, contact the published Security incident channel for a safe submission method. Include the time and whether you interacted with the message. Do not copy passwords, codes, or sensitive attachments into a helpdesk ticket.

## Credentials entered on a suspicious page

Stop interacting with the page and contact Security immediately using a known support channel. From a trusted device, follow Security's directions to change the company password and revoke affected sessions. Explain whether a password, MFA code, or approval was provided, without sending the secret itself. A password change alone is not confirmation that the incident is resolved.

## Repeated unexpected MFA prompts

Deny unrequested prompts and report their timestamps to Security. Do not approve a request because the caller claims to be IT. If prompts began after an email link or attachment was opened, report that connection as part of the incident. Identity Support may assist Security with session revocation and authenticator re-enrollment after identity verification.
